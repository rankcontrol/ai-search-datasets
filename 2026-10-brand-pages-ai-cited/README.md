# Brand pages that ChatGPT and Google cited (October 2026)

On October 19, 2026 we ran two checks on which pages AI answers cite, meaning link as a source. In the first we asked ChatGPT and Google 48 questions about 24 brands, such as "is Gusto worth it," and sorted the 54 pages from the brands' own sites that the answers cited by page type. In the second we asked 12 general how-to and cost questions, such as "how to wash a rug at home," and counted who got cited in Google's AI Overviews (the AI-written answer above the results), in Google's top 10 results and in ChatGPT's answers.

- **Write-up:** [Content Creation for Brands: 0 of 54 Pages AI Cited Were How-To Posts](https://medium.com/@thesearchshift/content-creation-for-brands-0-of-54-pages-ai-cited-were-how-to-posts-2e07d664756c)

## Results

| Page type | Pages cited |
|---|---:|
| Help center and FAQ | 12 |
| Product and collection pages | 10 |
| Pricing pages | 7 |
| Comparison pages | 7 |
| Homepages | 5 |
| Warranty, returns and policies | 5 |
| Other | 8 |
| General how-to blog posts | 0 |

Help center and FAQ pages were the largest group, with 12 pages that included Rover's and TaskRabbit's fee articles. Pricing pages from Close, Copper, Mailchimp and Pipedrive added 7. Comparisons were the one blog format in the set: a single Google AI Overview on whether Copper is worth it cited 6 of Copper's own comparison posts, and none of the 54 pages taught a general how-to.

The second check, on 12 how-to questions, flipped the picture. Companies that sell something made up 74% of the 84 sources in the AI Overviews and 66% of the top 10 results, and 11 of the 12 overviews cited at least one brand. ChatGPT answered 7 of the 12 questions without citing any source. It searched for the other 5 and cited brand pages in 3 of them, such as HubSpot, Salesforce and Zoho for "what is a crm."

## Limits

48 brand questions about 24 brands and 12 how-to questions, both checks on October 19, 2026, so the counts are a snapshot and not a rate. The page type labels are ours, and the 74% counts companies that sell something, which we treat as brands. Only the tallies are here, not the row-level list.
